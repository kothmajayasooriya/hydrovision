📚 Understanding Your Water Quality Index

Every test you log on HydroVision is scored against four measurable parameters, each compared to a WHO-based safe limit. This page explains what each one means, why it matters, and how it feeds into your WQI score - so a number on the dashboard always has a story behind it.

⚗️ pH Level

Safe range: 6.5 – 8.5  |  Ideal: 7.0

pH measures how acidic or alkaline water is, on a scale of 0 (very acidic) to 14 (very alkaline). Pure water sits close to neutral at 7. When pH drifts too far in either direction, water becomes corrosive - it can leach metals like lead or copper out of old pipes, irritate skin and eyes, and make disinfection with chlorine far less effective.

Too acidic (< 6.5)

Often linked to acid rain, mine drainage, or peaty soils. Can cause a metallic taste and pipe corrosion.

Too alkaline (> 8.5)

Common near limestone-rich ground or after certain agricultural runoff. Can leave scale build-up and a soapy taste.

How HydroVision scores it

Because 7.0 is ideal, we measure how far a reading strays from neutral - not just whether it's "low" or "high".

🌫️ Turbidity

Safe limit: below 5 NTU

Turbidity is the cloudiness of water caused by suspended particles - silt, clay, algae, or organic matter. It's measured in Nephelometric Turbidity Units (NTU). Cloudy water isn't automatically dangerous, but high turbidity is a strong warning sign: it can shield harmful microorganisms from disinfection and often points to recent contamination such as runoff after heavy rain.

Where it comes from

Soil erosion, construction runoff, algal blooms, and disturbed sediment in rivers or wells.

Why it matters

Particles can carry bacteria and viruses, reducing the effectiveness of chlorination.

Quick field check

If you can't see a coin at the bottom of a clear glass of the water, turbidity is likely elevated.

🧂 Total Dissolved Solids (TDS)

Safe limit: below 500 mg/L

TDS measures the combined amount of minerals, salts, and metals dissolved in water - things like calcium, magnesium, sodium, and sulphates. A moderate amount is normal and even gives water its taste. But when TDS climbs too high, it can indicate industrial discharge, saltwater intrusion, or excessive fertiliser and road-salt runoff reaching the source.

Low TDS

Often tastes flat; can also mean over-purified water lacking beneficial minerals.

High TDS

Can cause a salty, bitter, or metallic taste and, over time, contribute to scale in appliances and pipes.

Common sources

Agricultural runoff, wastewater discharge, natural mineral deposits, and road salt in colder regions.

🌾 Nitrates

Safe limit: below 45 mg/L

Nitrates are a form of nitrogen most commonly linked to agricultural fertiliser, livestock waste, and septic system runoff seeping into groundwater. Unlike turbidity, elevated nitrates usually can't be seen, smelled, or tasted - which is exactly why community testing matters. High nitrate levels are especially dangerous for infants, where they can interfere with the blood's ability to carry oxygen.

Main sources

Fertiliser runoff, animal waste, and leaking septic tanks - especially in farming areas.

Health risk

Highest concern for infants under six months and pregnant people; long-term exposure is also studied for other effects.

Why test regularly

Nitrate levels can rise seasonally after fertiliser application or heavy rain, so one clean reading doesn't guarantee lasting safety.

🧮 How Your WQI Score Is Calculated

HydroVision uses the Weighted Arithmetic Water Quality Index method. Each of the four parameters above is compared against its WHO-based permissible limit and given a "quality rating." Parameters closer to their unsafe limit contribute more heavily to the final score. pH is treated specially, since it's measured as distance from the ideal value (7.0) rather than a simple ratio.
